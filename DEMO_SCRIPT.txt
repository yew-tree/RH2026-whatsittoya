==============================================================================
WhatsItToYa?  -  HACKATHON DEMO SCRIPT
"Stop. Check. Protect."
Target length: about 4 minutes 45 seconds (stay under 5:00)
==============================================================================

HOW TO READ THIS SCRIPT
  SPEAKER:          what to say out loud
  ACTION:           what to click, type, paste or show
  EXPECTED RESULT:  what the judges should see on screen

Everything below was run against the finished app before this script was
written. Nothing here depends on a feature that does not work.

BEFORE YOU GO ON (60 seconds of setup)
  1. In a terminal, in the project folder:   npm run dev
     Then open http://localhost:3000 in the browser.
  2. Open the "Check a Message" page once to confirm it loads.
  3. Copy the text in the "DEMO INPUT" section (near the bottom of this file)
     to your clipboard.
  4. Set the browser zoom to 100%, and keep the "Make it easier to read" bar
     on STANDARD text and NORMAL contrast (you switch it live in Part 8).
  5. Start on the HOME page.


==============================================================================
PART 1 - OPENING / HOOK                                        (about 0:30)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Start on the HOME page. Do not click anything yet.

SPEAKER:
  "Picture Margaret. She is 74. It is 9 in the morning and her phone buzzes.
  It is her bank. 'Fraud team. Someone is moving $3,480 out of your account
  right now. You have 30 minutes. Read us the code we just texted you, and do
  not tell anyone, because the criminals may be watching your family.'

  Her heart is pounding. Here is the thing: the dangerous part is not the
  link in that message. It is the feeling. The scammer is not hacking her
  computer. The scammer is hacking her emotions, and trying to make her act
  BEFORE she stops to verify.

  We built WhatsItToYa? for that exact moment."

ACTION:   Gesture at the screen.

EXPECTED RESULT:  The Home page: the name "WhatsItToYa?", the line
  "Stop. Check. Protect.", and "Scammers create pressure. We help you slow
  things down."

SPEAKER:
  "The name is the idea. When someone you did not expect asks for your money,
  your passwords, or your trust, stop and ask them: 'What's it to ya?' Why do
  you need this, and who are you really?

  Stop. Check. Protect. Three words Margaret can remember even when she is
  scared."


==============================================================================
PART 2 - THE PROBLEM                                           (about 0:20)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Stay on the Home page.

SPEAKER:
  "Socially engineered scams are hard to stop because the weakness is not
  technical. Scammers use the same handful of levers every time:
  URGENCY - act now.
  FEAR - something terrible will happen.
  AUTHORITY - I am your bank, the police, your boss.
  TRUST - I am your grandson.
  SECRECY - do not tell anyone.
  And EMOTION underneath all of it.

  A spam filter cannot see any of that. A frightened person often cannot
  either, in the moment. So our shield is built around four steps:
  Detect, Interrupt, Protect, Educate. Let me show you."

EXPECTED RESULT:  The "How it protects you" row of four step cards is visible
  on the Home page (Detect, Interrupt, Protect, Educate).


==============================================================================
PART 3 - LIVE DEMO: DETECT                                     (about 0:30)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Click "Check a Message" in the top navigation (or the big
          "Check a message" button on Home).
ACTION:   Click inside the large "Paste the message here" box.
ACTION:   Paste the DEMO INPUT text (Ctrl+V).
ACTION:   Click the big "Check this message" button.

SPEAKER:
  "This is a fake bank fraud alert I wrote. It is fictional, but it is built
  from real scams: a made-up bank, an urgent transfer, a code request, a
  'safe account', and a demand for secrecy. I paste the whole email, headers
  and all, and press Check."

EXPECTED RESULT:  A short "Checking your message..." panel appears for about
  a second. Then the analysis finishes.

SPEAKER (as the dialog appears, point at the three bullet points in it):
  "Our shield read it for social-engineering signals. Look at what it noticed
  right away: it is asking for a verification code, it is asking for a
  password, and it is asking me to move money. That is Detect. We will look
  at every signal in a moment. First, look at what just happened to the
  screen."


==============================================================================
PART 4 - LIVE DEMO: INTERRUPT                                  (about 0:40)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Do not click yet. Let the judges read the dialog.

EXPECTED RESULT:  A large dialog headed "Before you continue..." covers the
  page. It says "We noticed several warning signs. Take a moment before
  sending money or information." It lists three of the signals, then a red
  box: "Do not send money yet. Do not share any codes or passwords. Do not use
  the phone number or link in the message." Three big buttons: "Ask someone I
  trust", "Show me how to check this safely", "Show me what you noticed".

SPEAKER:
  "This is the interruption. We call it smart friction. Notice what it is
  not. It is not a banner Margaret has seen a hundred times. It does not
  appear on every email. It only appears when the shield finds meaningful
  risk, in this case a request for money and a code, combined with pressure
  and secrecy.

  That is the hackathon's own principle: too early creates frustration, too
  late means money is lost. If we warned about everything, people would learn
  to click past us. So we stay quiet for ordinary mail, and stop the person at
  the moment it matters, before they reply, click or pay.

  And the tone is calm. 'You have done nothing wrong. Checking first was a
  smart move.' We never blame the person."

EXPECTED RESULT:  Keyboard focus sits on the first button, "Ask someone I
  trust". (You can mention that Tab stays inside the dialog and Escape closes
  it.)


==============================================================================
PART 5 - LIVE DEMO: PROTECT                                    (about 0:35)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Click "Ask someone I trust".

EXPECTED RESULT:  The dialog closes and the page jumps to a card titled
  "Ask Someone I Trust". It contains a yellow note "Prototype note:
  WhatsItToYa? does not send anything for you", a box for the person's name,
  and a ready-written message that begins "Hi, I got a message that claims to
  be from a bank or card company and it wants me to share a verification
  code..." and ends "I will contact the organization using official details
  I find myself, not the ones in the message."

ACTION:   (Optional, 5 seconds) Click in "Who will you ask?" and type "Sarah".
          Point out that the message now starts "Hi Sarah,".
ACTION:   Click "Copy message".

EXPECTED RESULT:  The button turns to "Copied!" and a green line says "Copied.
  Now paste it into a text or email to someone you trust."

SPEAKER:
  "Scammers win by isolating people. 'Do not tell the tellers. Do not tell
  your family.' So our next step is the opposite: bring someone in.

  One tap writes a calm message for her to send to a daughter, a neighbor, a
  friend. To be honest about our prototype: it copies the message or opens
  her own email or text app, and she presses send. We do not send anything for
  her, and the card says so. The design is ready for a real trusted-contact
  notification later.

  Right below it is 'Check It Safely'. For a bank claim it says: call the
  number on the back of your card, or use the bank's official app. It also
  says plainly: never use the phone number or the link in the suspicious
  message."

ACTION:   Scroll down one screen to "Check It Safely".

EXPECTED RESULT:  A card with a red warning "Never use a phone number, email
  address, or link from the suspicious message to check it", a drop-down
  "Who do they say they are?" set to "My bank or card company", and numbered
  steps.


==============================================================================
PART 6 - LIVE DEMO: EDUCATE                                    (about 0:40)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Scroll up to the "What we noticed" card.
ACTION:   If it helps, point at one entry, for example "Asking you to send or
          move money" or "Asking you to keep this secret".

EXPECTED RESULT:  Each entry shows what we found, a quote from the message
  ("In the message: ..."), and "Why it matters: ...". A button reads "Show 7
  more warning signs".

SPEAKER:
  "A generic red warning teaches nothing. We explain WHY. See this one:
  'Asking you to send or move money.' It quotes the exact sentence from the
  email, then explains: real banks never ask you to move your money to a safe
  account. That is a known scam."

ACTION:   Scroll to "How this message tries to influence you".

EXPECTED RESULT:  Cards for Authority, Unusual payment, Secrecy, Pretending
  to be someone you know, Urgency and Fear, each with a plain explanation and
  a "What helps" line.

SPEAKER:
  "And it names the manipulation. Urgency: 'This message is pressuring you to
  act immediately. Scammers create urgency because slowing down gives you
  time to notice warning signs.' Margaret does not just see a warning. She
  learns the trick, so she can spot it next time with no app at all."

ACTION:   Click "Pause Before You Pay" in the top navigation.
ACTION:   Click "Yes" on: "Did someone contact you unexpectedly?",
          "Are they asking you to act immediately?" and
          "Are they asking you to keep this secret?".
ACTION:   Click "See what to do".

EXPECTED RESULT:  A red result: "HIGH RISK. PAUSE. DO NOT PAY YET. Verify the
  request independently." Below it, "Why we say that" explains each answer.

SPEAKER:
  "Not everyone has a message to paste. Maybe it is a phone call. Pause Before
  You Pay is ten big yes-or-no questions. Three yeses and we say: pause, do
  not pay yet, verify independently. Again, we never say 'this is a scam'. We
  say 'here is why you should check first'."

(OPTIONAL - ONLY IF YOU ARE UNDER 4:00:)
ACTION:   Click "Practice". Choose the answer "Hang up, then call my grandchild
          on the number I already have" in the grandparent scenario (answers
          appear in a different order each session; the scenarios come in a
          fixed order, the grandchild one is second).
SPEAKER:  "And 'Would You Trust This?' lets people practice with realistic
          make-believe scams. No scores to fail, just a kind explanation of
          the warning signs."


==============================================================================
PART 7 - THE FOUR HACKATHON QUESTIONS                          (about 0:30)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Stay on any page. Count the questions on your fingers.

SPEAKER:
  "The challenge asks four questions. Here are our answers.

  WHAT DOES OUR SHIELD NOTICE?
  The pressure, not just the link. Urgency, threats, secrecy, fake authority,
  and requests for codes, passwords, gift cards, crypto or wire transfers,
  plus lookalike senders and risky links.

  WHEN DOES IT INTERVENE?
  Only when real risk shows up, like a money or code request combined with
  pressure. Ordinary email gets no interruption.

  WHAT HAPPENS NEXT?
  A pause, a message to someone they trust, and exact steps to verify
  independently, never using the contact details in the message.

  WHY WOULD THE USER LISTEN?
  Because we are calm, we use plain words, we explain why, and we never blame
  them."


==============================================================================
PART 8 - WHY OLDER ADULTS / ACCESSIBILITY                      (about 0:25)
==============================================================================

ACTION:   Click "A++" in the "Make it easier to read" bar at the top, then
          click "High contrast".

EXPECTED RESULT:  All text becomes much larger and the colors switch to black
  on white with heavy borders. The choice is remembered on this device.

SPEAKER:
  "We did not design this for people who cannot cope. We designed it because
  manipulation is sophisticated, and anyone can be caught on a bad day. The
  tools are simple: large text by default, one-click bigger text and high
  contrast, big buttons with real words on them instead of mystery icons,
  short sentences, keyboard and screen-reader support, and an option for less
  motion. No jargon, no scores to decode. Just: what we noticed, why it
  matters, and what to do next."

ACTION:   Click "A" and "High contrast" again to switch back to normal.


==============================================================================
PART 9 - TECHNICAL EXPLANATION                                 (about 0:20)
==============================================================================

SPEAKER:
  "Quick look under the hood. It is a web app built with React and
  TypeScript, with a small Node server. The shield's rules run right in the
  browser: it looks for scam wording, checks links and whether the sender
  really matches the company, then turns that into one of three plain levels:
  Low Concern, Be Careful, High Risk. Because those rules are local, it still
  works with no internet and no AI.

  If a Gemini key is configured, the server can add an AI second opinion on
  top. And the app never says a message is 'safe'. The lowest level is 'Low
  Concern', with a reminder to verify anything about money."

==============================================================================

ACTION:   Click "Home" so the name and tagline are on screen.

SPEAKER:

  secrecy, the countdown clock, BEFORE someone hands over their money, their
  passwords, or their trust.

  Because the scammer needs Margaret to act fast.
  We give her three words to slow down.

  Stop. Check. Protect.
  WhatsItToYa?"


==============================================================================
DEMO BACKUP PLAN
==============================================================================

IF THE AI / API IS UNAVAILABLE
  Nothing to do. This demo does not need it. With no AI key, or if the AI
  call fails, the app automatically uses its built-in checks. The DEMO INPUT
  produces the same High Risk result and the same interruption. Tell the
  judges: "This is our offline mode, which is also what protects someone with
  no connection." (Open "More details (optional)" on the result page to show
  the line "How we checked".)
  NOTE: the AI path was not tested with a live key while this script was
  written. Do NOT promise AI features in the demo unless you tested them with
  your own key beforehand.

IF INTERNET ACCESS FAILS
  The app runs from your own laptop (http://localhost:3000), so the demo
  still works. The only outside item is the Google font, and the app falls
  back to the computer's normal font. The FTC link in the footer will not
  open; do not click it. Do not open any message link (the demo avoids it).

IF AN ANALYSIS TAKES TOO LONG
  The app gives up on the AI after 12 seconds and switches to the built-in
  checks by itself. Keep talking about the problem while it works. If it
  still seems stuck after 15 seconds, click "Clear and start over", then click
  the "Fake bank fraud alert" example card (no pasting needed).

IF A FEATURE FAILS DURING JUDGING
  - Pasting does not work: use the example cards on the Check page ("Fake
    bank fraud alert") or the "See an example scam" button on the Home page.
  - Copy button blocked by the browser: a red line tells you; select the text
    in the message box and press Ctrl+C. Or just read it aloud.
  - Dialog does not appear: the result page still shows "HIGH RISK" and the
    stop rules at the top. Say "this is where we interrupt" and continue.
  - The page looks broken: refresh (F5) and use an example card.
  - The server will not start: close the terminal, run "npm run dev" again.
    As a last resort run "npm run build" and show the project on screen.

WHAT STILL WORKS COMPLETELY LOCALLY, WITH NO INTERNET AND NO AI
  - Check a Message: analysis, risk level, interruption, signals, tactics
  - Ask Someone I Trust (copy message; email and text buttons open the
    computer's own apps)
  - Check It Safely guides
  - Pause Before You Pay
  - Learn to Spot a Scam (14 scam guides and 8 manipulation tactics)
  - Would You Trust This? (6 scenarios)
  - Text size, high contrast, spacing and motion options

IF YOU ARE RUNNING LONG
  Skip the optional Practice step and the "Sarah" typing step. Cut the Part 9
  technical section to one sentence: "React and TypeScript, with rules that
  run offline in the browser."

30-SECOND CHEAT SHEET
==============================================================================

HOOK:       Margaret, 74. "Fraud team" texts: read us the code, move your money,
            tell no one, you have 30 minutes. The danger is not the link. It is
            the pressure.
PROBLEM:    Scams work by manipulating emotion (urgency, fear, authority,
            trust, secrecy) so people act BEFORE they verify.
SOLUTION:   WhatsItToYa? helps you slow down, spot the warning signs, and
            verify before you act. "What's it to ya?" = why do you need this?

DETECT:     Paste the DEMO INPUT, click "Check this message". Shield reads the
            pressure plus code, password and money requests. Result: HIGH RISK.
INTERRUPT:  "Before you continue..." dialog. Only for real risk. Too early =
            frustration, too late = money lost. Calm, no blame.
PROTECT:    "Ask Someone I Trust" writes a message to copy (prototype, sends
            nothing). "Check It Safely": never use the number or link in the
            message.
EDUCATE:    "What we noticed" + "Why it matters" + named tactics (urgency,
            fear, secrecy...). Then Pause Before You Pay: PAUSE. DO NOT PAY YET.

4 QUESTIONS: Notice = pressure and payment/code requests. Intervene = only on
            real risk. Next = pause, ask someone, verify independently. Listen
            = calm, plain words, explains why, never blames.
ACCESSIBILITY: Click A++ and High contrast. Large text, big labeled buttons,
            plain words, keyboard friendly.
TECH:       React + TypeScript, small Node server, rules run offline in the
            browser. Optional Gemini AI on top if a key is set.

CLOSING:    "The goal is not to catch one more phishing email. It is to
            interrupt the manipulation before someone loses money, passwords,
            or trust. Stop. Check. Protect. WhatsItToYa?"

NEVER SAY: "100% safe", "guaranteed", "catches all scams".
